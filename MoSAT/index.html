Abstract

Human motion is shaped by both the surrounding environment and behavioral intent. Spatial audio provides directional and temporal cues for a response, while language describes the desired action and its style. MoSAT studies human motion generation jointly conditioned on these complementary signals.

We introduce STAM, a dataset that extends SAM's paired spatial audio and human motion with detailed, motion-aligned textual descriptions. MoSAT combines a motion variational autoencoder with a latent flow-matching framework, using hierarchical attention to bring natural-language intent and spatial-audio cues into the motion generation process. We also develop tri-modal evaluators to measure alignment between the multimodal conditions and generated motion. Experiments evaluate motion quality, condition alignment, inference-time editing, and an application to simulated humanoid control.
